MANUSCRIPT

Some passages of Scripture refuse to leave the human heart alone.

Some books are written to defend positions. Others are written to challenge them. This book was written for something far more personal and, to me, far more sacred.

This book was written because certain passages of Scripture refused to leave my heart alone.

As a teacher, I have spent years helping people slow down enough to truly see what stands before them instead of rushing toward assumptions. As a minister, I have watched wounded people quietly wrestle with questions they were terrified to speak aloud in church spaces. As a father, I understand the deep ache of wanting the people you love to grow up in truth instead of fear. As a believer, I hold Scripture with reverence, knowing the Word of God deserves honesty, patience, and humility from those who approach it. As a gay man, I know what it means to sit inside conversations where people debate whether love, belonging, tenderness, and faithfulness can fully exist within someone like you while never realizing there are human hearts listening from the pews.

All of those parts of my life walk together through these pages.

This book did not begin with rebellion against God, nor with a desire to tear apart Scripture in pursuit of modern approval. The journey began with reading the Bible carefully and noticing how quickly certain passages created discomfort once readers slowed down long enough to truly hear them.

The relationship between David and Jonathan has remained preserved within Scripture for thousands of years with astonishing emotional depth. Their covenant, their loyalty, their grief, their tears, their embraces, and David’s lament after Jonathan’s death continue to stand among the most intimate passages found anywhere in sacred literature.

Generation after generation has preached these verses, celebrated them, softened them, debated them, defended them, and sometimes hurried past them altogether. Yet the language remains. Scripture itself preserves their story with remarkable tenderness and without apology.

Modern readers often approach David and Jonathan carrying assumptions inherited from culture, denominational tradition, fear surrounding sexuality, discomfort with emotional vulnerability between men, and ideas about masculinity that would have been foreign to much of the ancient world.

Ancient covenant relationships carried immense social, spiritual, emotional, and political significance. Public weeping between men was not automatically viewed with suspicion. Expressions of devotion and loyalty often possessed a depth modern readers struggle to recognize because contemporary culture has trained many people to fear tenderness long before they ever encounter it honestly.

This book does not seek scandal, controversy, or outrage. My goal has never been to shock the church or force conclusions onto readers. My goal is honesty.

Honest engagement with the Hebrew text. Honest engagement with translation history. Honest engagement with church interpretation. Honest engagement with the emotional language Scripture itself chose to preserve.

Truth never fears examination.
