Wounded Readers

Beyond theology, interpretation, and debate stand real people whose lives were shaped by the fear surrounding tenderness, belonging, identity, and faith. This section turns carefully toward them.

“Fear based theology rarely wounds abstract people. Fear wounds sons, daughters, families, and souls.”

PART INTRODUCTION

Some wounds remain hidden beneath silence for years.

Some books are written only for scholars, pastors, or theologians. This book was also written for the people who sat quietly in church pews carrying questions they believed might cost them belonging if spoken aloud.

By this point in the journey, the emotional depth surrounding David and Jonathan can no longer be dismissed easily. The covenant remains visible. The tenderness remains visible. The grief remains visible. Yet beyond every historical debate and theological argument stand real human beings whose lives were shaped by the way these passages were interpreted inside church spaces.

This section turns toward them.

Here we move beyond translation, commentary, and interpretation alone and begin examining the spiritual wounds fear based theology often leaves behind. Many LGBTQIA+ believers grew up loving God deeply while simultaneously learning to fear honesty about themselves. Many learned to separate survival from authenticity because certain questions, emotions, or forms of tenderness immediately created danger within religious environments.

Some remained silent for years.

Some walked away entirely.

Some stayed while grieving quietly inside spaces that preached love yet often struggled to offer safety.

This part of the book does not exist to attack the church. The church has also been home, healing, worship, family, and sacred encounter for countless people, including me. Yet honesty requires acknowledging that wounded believers are not theoretical discussions. They are sons, daughters, parents, ministers, worship leaders, teachers, and faithful people who spent years trying to reconcile their love for God with the fear surrounding their existence.

Within these chapters, we will explore spiritual trauma, belonging, identity, silence, dignity, and the emotional cost of living beneath fear driven theology. We will also ask difficult questions about what happens when tenderness becomes treated as dangerous before compassion has a chance to speak.

The goal is not bitterness.

The goal is healing.

Because behind every theological conversation are human hearts still searching for places where truth and love can exist together without fear demanding one be sacrificed for the other.
