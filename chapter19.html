Chapter 19

The God Who Never Feared Love

Perhaps the deepest question beneath this entire journey was never whether love existed, but whether God Himself ever feared the tenderness humanity carried toward one another.

“Perfect love does not survive by fear. Perfect love casts fear out.”

CHAPTER NINETEEN

Love remained the center of Scripture long before fear became the center of interpretation.

Fear has shaped much of this journey.

Fear shaped interpretation. Fear shaped silence. Fear shaped theology, churches, relationships, and wounded lives. Fear taught generations of believers that honesty could become dangerous once love no longer fit comfortably inside approved expectations.

Yet fear is not the center of Scripture.

Love is.

From the opening pages of the Bible to the final promises of redemption, Scripture continually reveals a God moving toward humanity rather than away from it. God enters grief. God enters covenant. God enters suffering. God enters brokenness. Again and again throughout Scripture, divine love reaches toward people carrying shame, fear, exile, weakness, failure, loneliness, and longing.

That movement matters deeply.

Many wounded believers grew up imagining God primarily through the fear surrounding them. Some learned to associate holiness with rejection rather than restoration. Some learned to believe God remained constantly disappointed, distant, or waiting for failure. Others spent years praying terrified prayers, hoping love from heaven might still exist for them somewhere beneath the fear handed to them by people on earth.

Fear distorts the face of God when fear becomes louder than compassion.

Yet throughout the Gospels, Jesus continually moved toward the very people religious systems often kept at a distance. He touched lepers others avoided. He defended the shamed. He ate beside the rejected. He spoke peace over the fearful. He treated wounded people as human beings before treating them as theological discussions.

That is not weakness. That is holiness expressed through love.

Many believers were taught that love and truth stand opposed to one another, yet Scripture repeatedly presents them together. Truth without love becomes cruelty. Love without truth becomes directionless. Christ embodied both fully without fear controlling the way He encountered broken people.

This chapter does not attempt to remove every theological disagreement surrounding David and Jonathan, sexuality, or interpretation. Honest believers still wrestle sincerely with difficult questions. Some readers will continue reaching different conclusions after finishing this book.

What matters here is something deeper.

No wounded person should have to fear being honest before God.

No believer should feel forced to disappear internally in order to survive spiritually. No human heart should be treated as disposable in the pursuit of theological certainty. No one searching sincerely for God should encounter fear more quickly than compassion inside communities claiming to follow Christ.

The story of David and Jonathan continues carrying such emotional power because Scripture preserved tenderness without shame surrounding it. Their covenant remains visible. Their grief remains visible. Their devotion remains visible. Even after centuries of debate, their relationship still stands within sacred text with emotional honesty intact.

Perhaps that honesty reflects something larger about God Himself.

Perhaps divine love was never as frightened of human tenderness as many religious systems became. Perhaps sacred love was always meant to draw people closer to truth rather than deeper into fear. Perhaps the God who created human hearts never intended fear to become the loudest voice guiding them toward Him.

“There is no fear in love; but perfect love casteth out fear.”

— 1 John 4:18

Those words do not describe shallow love. They describe love powerful enough to remove terror from the relationship between humanity and God. Perfect love does not humiliate honesty. Perfect love does not survive by forcing wounded people into silence. Perfect love does not depend upon fear in order to maintain authority.

Perfect love casts fear out.

That truth reaches beyond theology into the deepest parts of spiritual life itself. People heal where love creates safety for honesty. People breathe again where fear no longer controls belonging. People encounter God more clearly where compassion remains stronger than shame.

Many LGBTQIA+ believers have spent years searching for that kind of spiritual safety. Some are still searching even now. Some still sit quietly in church pews wondering whether love and faith can truly exist together without fear tearing them apart. Some still open Scripture hoping to encounter not condemnation first, but mercy.

Perhaps this journey began there all along.

Not with rebellion. Not with scandal. Not with the desire to dismantle faith.

Perhaps this journey began with wounded people searching Scripture for the face of a God who never feared love in the first place.

Fear taught many people to hide from God. Love keeps inviting them closer anyway.
